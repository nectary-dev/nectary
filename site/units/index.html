Nectary units

Contributors can be paid in units of a service instead of a capped revenue claim. Units can be sent to other holders and are burned when spent. Nectary is the first seed repo: its own tickets are the work, and this ledger is public.

1 unit = 1,000 Zenod memory operations (one operation = one memory saved or recalled)
Not spendable yet: Zenod's team agreed in principle to be the service units are spent on. Turning it on costs money, so it waits for Jordi's yes. Until then units are counted and can be sent, but buy nothing.

✓ Ledger checks pass: hash chain intact, caps held, no negative balances, 1 transfer each covered by the sender's balance, every award cites a done ticket.

Risk, said plainly. Units that can be sent can also be sold between holders. That raises the US securities and EU MiCA risk the research, section 0 described, which found units low-risk only while they could not be sold. We keep it small: the project never sells or lists units, never talks about their price, and keeps them off-chain in this ledger. An on-chain token or exchange listing waits for a lawyer's answer to whether transferable units like these are a security or need a MiCA white paper. Not legal advice.

The rules

  1. Priced in a serviceOne unit buys 1,000 Zenod memory operations. Never a price in dollars or euros.
  2. Only for accepted ticketsUnits are awarded when a ticket is accepted as done, never per line or pull request.
  3. Rate fixed before workEach ticket's units are written in the ledger before anyone starts it.
  4. Your choice per ticketTake units, or a capped revenue claim repaid at cost. One or the other, never both.
  5. Public cap per epicEach epic can issue only the total written in the ledger.
  6. Transferable between holdersA holder can send units to anyone. Never more than they hold, never to themselves.
  7. Burned when spentSpending destroys the units, so supply only shrinks with use.
  8. Never sold or listed by usThe project never sells units, never lists them on an exchange, never talks about their price.
  9. Same rules for the teamThe team's own units follow the same rates and caps.
